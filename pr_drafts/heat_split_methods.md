# Methods text for Sections 2.4.2 and 2.7

We classified temperature-attributable deaths into five temperature ranges. The minimum mortality temperature (MMT) was obtained from each city- and age-specific exposure-response function and held fixed in absolute temperature. Temperatures below the MMT were divided at the 2.5th percentile into extreme and moderate cold. Temperatures above the MMT were divided into moderate heat, normal-extreme heat and extreme heat. The static 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles were estimated from ERA5-Land daily mean temperature in 2000-2014, the historical period used by Masselot et al. for calibrating the climate-model series. The exposure-response basis knots and boundaries, and the rule used to identify the MMT, remained based on the full 1990-2019 ERA5-Land series, following the source study.

For each city, general circulation model (GCM), scenario and projected year t, we estimated a moving 97.5th percentile from the calibrated daily GCM temperatures during the 15 preceding years, t-15 to t-1. The current year was therefore never included. Because quantile calibration did not make the empirical 97.5th percentile of every historical GCM series exactly equal to the corresponding ERA5-Land percentile, we anchored the moving threshold to the observed baseline. Specifically, the threshold for year t was the ERA5-Land 2000-2014 P97.5 plus the difference between the calibrated GCM P97.5 in years t-15 to t-1 and that GCM's calibrated P97.5 in 2000-2014. This implements the same observed-baseline-plus-simulated-change principle as Masselot's temperature calibration while retaining the model-specific climate-change signal. Consequently, the 2015 moving threshold equals the observed static threshold, because its window is exactly 2000-2014. Observed temperatures from 2015-2019 were not spliced into the scenario series. When the anchored moving threshold fell below the static P97.5, it was clamped to the static value.

Moderate heat comprised temperatures from the MMT to the static P97.5; normal-extreme heat comprised temperatures above the static P97.5 and below the anchored moving P97.5; and extreme heat comprised temperatures at or above the anchored moving P97.5. If the MMT exceeded the static heat threshold, all heat above the MMT was classified as extreme. In the without-climate-change branch, the moving threshold was fixed at the static P97.5, making the normal-extreme band empty by construction. This three-way heat classification is a study-specific extension of Masselot et al.; all other temperature calibration and attributable-number calculations followed their framework.

We constructed annual age- and cause-specific mortality schedules for ages 65 to 100+ and calculated remaining life expectancy at age 65 (LE65) and lifespan inequality conditional on survival to age 65 (LI65+). Horiuchi decompositions allocated changes in each indicator to single years of age and to six mutually exclusive causes: the five temperature ranges and all remaining deaths. The primary analysis compared consecutive 5-year-period mean mortality schedules, so reported contributions describe changes in 5-year-period means rather than changes between individual weather years. We used 400 Horiuchi steps. Annual decompositions can be reproduced by setting `DECOMP_ANNUAL=1`, but were not used for the paper figures. All reported results are central estimates without uncertainty intervals.
